# The first human test — script

**Written 4 September 2026, to be run when three people are found.**

Nobody has used this site. Twenty-two rounds of instruments have checked whether it is
*correct*; none can tell whether it *works*. This is a script so that the hour is spent
watching, not deciding what to ask.

Three participants, twenty minutes each. **A teacher, a student, a writer** — one of the
roles the site claims to serve, so a failure is traceable to a promise.

Their own device. The live URL, sent as a link, nothing else:
`https://mojtaba-alehosseini.github.io/learn-claude/`

---

## Before they arrive

Nothing to install, nothing to sign. One sentence when you send the link:

> "I'll ask you to do four small things on a website. Say whatever you're thinking — if
> something is confusing, that's the useful part."

Then stop talking.

---

## The four tasks

Each is tied to a promise the site makes. Read them aloud, as written. They are worded as
a person would hear them, not as we would write them.

**1. "Find the one thing you'd open first, for you."**
→ *The promise: the two front-door questions lead somewhere personal.*
Watch whether they answer both questions, whether they see the picks block at all, and
whether they pick from it or scroll past it.

**2. "There's a line on these cards called 'Skip if'. What do you think it's for?"**
→ *The promise: the skip line is the product, not a footnote.*
Ask only after they have been on a card for a while. If they have not read one, that is
the finding.

**3. "Find something about Cowork — without using the search box."**
→ *The promise: filters and topics work, and search is not the only way in.*
Watch which route they take: topic filter, browsing, a path, the home page questions.

**4. "One of these cards says 'Read by AI'. What does that tell you?"**
→ *The promise: the tier ladder is honest and legible.*
Whatever they say is the answer. If they think it means a person checked it, the ladder
has failed and the fix is copy, not another badge.

---

## What to watch, not ask

Write these down while they work. Do not ask about them — asking creates the answer.

- **Time to first click.** How long the home page holds them before they do anything.
- **Where they hesitate.** The pause before a click is the finding; the click is not.
- **Do they read `Skip if` at all?** Eyes on that line, unprompted, before task 2.
- **Is the picks block noticed?** Do they say anything about "Start with these three", or
  does it read as more cards?
- **Do they see "picked by AI"?** And if they do, does it change what they trust?
- **What they think the tier labels mean**, in their words. Not whether they match ours.
- **How they leave.** Through our resource page to the real thing, or straight out of the
  site? Straight out means the resource page is a speed bump.
- **Anything they try that does not exist.** A sort, a save, a back button that loses
  their filters. The gaps they reach for are worth more than the paths they take.

---

## What we do not do

- **No leading.** "Did you notice the…" invents the memory. Ask "what were you thinking
  there?" instead.
- **No explaining.** If they ask what something means, say "what would you guess?" and
  write down the guess. The guess is the data.
- **No rescuing.** Let them be stuck for a full thirty seconds before helping. Being stuck
  is the finding.
- **No tracking.** The site has no analytics by design and this test adds none. Paper and
  a pen. Nothing is recorded, nothing is stored, and the notes hold no names.
- **No fixing during the session.** Write it down; the next person is a fresh reading.

## What counts as a finding

Not "they didn't like it". A finding is **a place where the site's behaviour and a
person's expectation came apart**, written as: what they did, what they expected, what
happened instead.

Three or more people hitting the same wall is a pattern. One person hitting it is a
question. Both get written down; only the first gets acted on without asking.

Findings go in **`docs/attack/HUMAN-1.md`**, the same shape as the ten role attacks:
what was tested, what broke, what it cost, what changes. One file for all three
participants — the comparison between them is most of the value.

---

## Two hours, honestly

Sixty minutes of sessions, thirty of writing up, thirty for the fixes that are obvious
enough to make immediately. Anything larger becomes a normal round with a brief.

If only one person can be found, run it anyway. One reading beats none, and this document
exists so the hour is not spent deciding what to ask.
