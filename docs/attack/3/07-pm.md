# Attack 3: a product manager
Written as a product manager, 2026-09-06. Site version: 9deb0db.

## 1. The first 60 seconds

The headline is a sentence with two blanks in it: **"I'm a [role] and I've [level]."** I like
that. It tells me in one line what the site is going to do.

Then I sat still and watched. The doodle on the right changed four times — a share icon, a
mug, a microscope, an easel — and a different role chip lit up each time. The sentence never
moved. I sampled the headline element eight times over fourteen seconds and got one value
back, every time: `I'm a [role] and I've [level].` So the loop animates a picture and a
highlight, and leaves the one thing on the page that would show me what the site does still
reading `[role]` and `[level]`. It looks like a page that failed to load its own data.

The moment I clicked **a product manager** the sentence filled in and the second question
appeared. That is a good interaction. It just does not advertise itself.

Under the fold: "588 resources, each with a reason to skip it and the date we last looked."
That is the promise. Three columns follow — "We say when to skip", "We say how we checked",
"We show the date." Fine. No pricing, no signup, no cookie banner. I was on Browse in under
a minute. As a front door it is better than most things I am shown in a vendor demo.

**never used Claude (12).** Thin, and it drifts. The first card under the picks is
"AI Can't Replace Real Research in Empathy Mapping" from Nielsen Norman Group. Its own
`Skip if:` says: *"There is also no tool in it - no Claude, no prompts, no screenshots - so
pair it with something that shows the mechanics."* So the first non-pick item handed to a
person who has never opened Claude, on a site called Learn Claude, is an article with no
Claude in it. Two more of the twelve are the Help Center pages "Choose a Claude plan" and
"How do usage and length limits work?" — price tables, not learning. All twelve badges read
`Skimmed`.

**used it a little (40).** This is where I actually am, and it is the best cell. But two of
the three picks are Claude Code — a terminal tool — and the third is an Anthropic plugin
that needs Cowork or Claude Code and MCP connectors.

**used it a lot (54).** **16 of the 54 titles contain the words "Claude Code."** Two of the
three picks are Claude Code again. Across all 112 PM resources, **23 titles contain "Claude
Code" and 27 contain "Cowork."**

**built things with it (6).** Six items. Three of them are called "the three." Of the six:
two GitHub repos, one MCP video whose own `For:` line says *"Developers and technical product
managers"*, a $3,000 course, a 15-minute docs page about writing voice, and one card nobody
has opened. `builder` for a PM does not mean "you built something." It means "you can install
things from GitHub."

**Verdict on the front door.** It works mechanically at all four levels. What it answers,
at three of the four, is a question I did not ask: *how do I learn Claude Code?*

## 3. What the catalogue actually gives me (are these really for me?)

Some of it, genuinely yes. "Product management on the AI exponential" is Anthropic's own
Claude Code product lead describing how her team splits work. "Feedback synthesis to
prioritized themes", "PRD from a problem statement", "Thread to decision doc", "Launch
readiness sweep", "Competitive teardown and positioning gaps" — those are my week, named in
my words. That is the good half.

The other half is not mine. Two examples I can point at:

- **"Using Claude Cowork for legal: answer fast questions on past decisions"** sits in
  `role=pm`. Its own `For:` line reads: *"Legal or adjacent teams (the page also names
  support, compliance, finance and engineering) who keep getting the same 'what did we decide
  on X' questions."* Product is not in that list. Nowhere.
- **"Using Claude Cowork for marketing ops: run a weekly review that preps itself"** is also
  in `role=pm`. Its `For:` line: *"A marketing ops person who runs the same weekly metrics
  review by hand."*

These are the collection cards. As a PM I read both, worked out neither was for me, and moved
on — about forty seconds each. They do not confuse me. They just cost me time, twice, in a
list where the whole product promise is that somebody already spent that time for me. The
rule that a role tag has to be true of the card did not fire on either one, and the evidence
that it should have is printed on the card.

**Does `builder` mean anything to a PM?** No. See section 2. Six items, of which one — the
Every guide — is about changing how the job works. The rest are install instructions and a
$3,000 certification.

**Do the five collection cards help or confuse?** Neither. They are legible — I understood
what "Using Claude Cowork for legal" was before clicking. They are simply filed under the
wrong person.

## 4. Paths

There **is** a path for me: **"Product work without waiting for engineering"**, 5 steps,
about 2 hours, free. It is the best thing on this site and I want to say so before the rest.

Its intro is the single most useful sentence anywhere here:

> "Steps 1 to 4 need nothing but a browser — stop after step 4 and you can already synthesise
> research, keep it somewhere, delegate the repeatable parts and know what is safe to paste
> in. Step 5 needs a terminal, and it is fair to decide that is not your job."

And step 5's own note: *"This step needs a terminal, and if that is a no then stop here — the
four above stand on their own... the PM shelf leans heavily on Claude Code; this is the
gentlest way in."*

The site knows about my complaint in section 2. It wrote it down. On one page.

Three problems:

1. **It is a beginner path and it does not say so.** The header reads "For a product manager"
   with no level. Every one of the five steps comes out of the `used it a little` pool. There
   is no path for `used it a lot` (54 resources, my largest cell) and none for `built things
   with it`. Seven paths exist; none is pitched above the start.
2. **A PM is never shown it.** On `browse.html?role=pm&level=confident` the only path link on
   the entire page is *"Step 5 of 5 in Judging AI's design output"* — a designer's path. On
   `browse.html?role=pm&level=never-used` the only path link is *"Step 2 of 5 in Judging AI's
   design output"* — the same designer's path. At two of four levels, the only route the site
   advertises to a product manager belongs to somebody else.
3. **Answering the two questions never mentions Paths.** "Show me" goes to a 54-card list. It
   does not go to, or link to, the two-hour ordered route written for exactly the person who
   just answered those two questions.

I followed it. Steps 1 to 4 are correct and correctly ordered, and the reason given for each
position ("Fourth, and deliberately not first. Safety guidance is abstract until you know what
you would actually delegate") is real editorial work. I would do this path.

## 5. The card and the resource page

I opened three resource pages.

**"Product management on the AI exponential"** (Anthropic). Badge `Read by AI`. Summary, "What
it teaches" in three bullets, "Who it's for", "Skip it if", "Before this — Daily experience
using Claude", `Checked 5 Sep 2026 · Published 19 Mar 2026 · Updated 20 Aug 2026 · Found
through Anthropic`. **Yes, I clicked through.** This page did its job.

**"Production Ready Agentic AI PM Certification and Claude Cert Prep (Maven)"**, `from $3,000`.
**No.** Its own `Skip it if` told me not to: *"You want to learn Claude for PM craft. Most of
this is AI product management, and Claude is the vehicle."* Which is what I want. So the site
recommended it to me and then told me to skip it, on the same card.

**"Ralph: PRD skill plus autonomous implementation loop"**, badge `Found only`. The stripped
card. Title, publisher, `code / half a day / free`, one line: *"Skip if: You want something we
have read. We have not opened this one yet."* **No, I did not click through.**

**Do the stripped cards read as honest or broken?** Honest. The badge, the skip line and the
"How we checked this one" block all say the same thing, and nothing pretends. But it is not
clean: the card still asserts `half a day` for a page nobody has read, and it is still filed
under `pm` and `builder` — three claims made about a page nobody opened. And it exposes a hole
in the tagging rule: a rule that drops a role tag when the `who_for` line denies it cannot fire
on a card that has no `who_for` line. This card has none, so its `pm` tag was never tested.

**The card and the tier badge.** On a card the badge is a `<span>` with the definition in a
`title=` attribute and no `tabindex`. It is not a link, not a button, not focusable. You cannot
reach it with a keyboard, and on a phone there is no hover, so on a phone you cannot reach it
at all. In the mobile filter sheet the "How well checked" group offers Read in full / Read by
AI / Skimmed / Found only with no definitions and no link — I checked: the sheet contains zero
links. The definitions do exist, on the resource page and on How we check. But not from a card.

**Does naming one person and Claude as "we" raise or lower my trust?** **Raises it, a lot.**
How we check says: one person, a researcher at the Technical University of Denmark, working on
it outside their job; Claude does the reading; *"A person sets the rules... and has not yet read
a single resource end to end, which is why the strongest label has the count you can see above
it."* And: *"open an issue. It needs a GitHub account, which is a real barrier and we know it."*
I have read a hundred "curated directories" that would not admit either. This is the reason I
would send the site to somebody.

It also raises the stakes on section 9, because the same page says Claude *"chooses the three
picks."*

**Do the date lines make the site look maintained?** Yes. `Checked 5 Sep 2026` is yesterday.
Picks are stamped `picked by AI · 6 Sep 2026` — today. Stale items carry *"over a year ago, may
not match Claude today"*. That reads maintained. What undercuts it: **78 of my 112 cards say
"No publish date given"** (442 of 588 site-wide, per STATUS.md). And on "Claude 101 (DataCamp)"
the card says `Checked 18 Aug 2026` with no caveat, while the project's own STATUS.md files
datacamp.com under hosts that block the weekly check, "last confirmed by a person: never". The
site has a mechanism for printing caveats beside a date. It did not use it here.

**Is it clear what I would pay before I click?** For the $3,000 course, yes — `pay once` and
`from $3,000` on the card. For two others, no, and it is worse than unclear. See finding 4.

## 7. On a phone

I set 375 x 812 and went through Browse at `role=pm&level=confident`.

**What works.** No horizontal scroll — document width 375, viewport width 375, and I found no
element overflowing the viewport. The desktop filter rail collapses cleanly and a fixed
"Open filters" bar sits at the bottom of the screen. It opens a proper sheet with all eight
groups — Role, Level, Time, Topic, Format, Cost, How well checked, Source — the current
selections correctly pre-ticked (`roles:pm`, `levels:confident`), and a "Show 54 resources"
button that counts as you tick. That is a well-built mobile filter. I expected it to be broken
and it is not.

**What does not.** Two things.

- The tier badge. `title=` only, on a non-focusable span. No hover on a phone. The one place
  the badge appears in a tappable form — the "How well checked" group in the sheet — has no
  definitions and no link out. On a phone the four badges are decoration.
- Length. 54 cards at 375px wide is a 32,199-pixel page, about 40 phone screens, with no
  pagination and no "back to top". Each card carries `For:` and `Skip if:` in full. On a
  desktop that density is the point. On a train it is a scroll I gave up on.

## 10. Everything that is broken, ranked (evidence for each)

> "0 resources for a product manager
> We have not covered this role yet. It's on the list. Browse everything instead."

588, per STATUS.md and stated on How we check. Not PM-specific: `browse.html?role=developer&checked=reviewed`

produces the correct message: *"Nothing matches all of those. Try removing one filter — time is

## 12. What is genuinely good (honest, brief)

- **"Product work without waiting for engineering."** Five steps, right order, a reason for each
  position, and an explicit permission to stop at step 4 if the terminal is not your job. Nothing
  else I have used tells me where to stop.
- **How we check.** One person at DTU, Claude does the reading, "has not yet read a single resource
  end to end", GitHub is "a real barrier and we know it". No directory admits any of that.
- **`Skip if:` on every card.** When it is a real condition it is the most useful line on the page,
  and it repeatedly talked me out of things — which is the point.
- **The picks reasons are comparative.** "The only candidate about changing how the job works
  rather than doing one task faster." That is a judgement against the rest of the pool, not a blurb.
- **The mobile filter sheet.** Eight groups, state pre-ticked, a live "Show 54 resources" button.
  Better than most shipped products.
- **The stale flag.** *"over a year ago, may not match Claude today"* beside a date, in words.
- **No accounts, no tracking, no cookie banner, no email gate.** I got to a filtered list in under
  a minute.

## 13. Would I come back, and what one thing would make me?

Yes — but for one page, not for the site. I would bookmark "Product work without waiting for
engineering" and I would send the How-we-check page to anyone who asked me why I trusted it. The
rest I would use once, the way you use a search result, and not return to.

The one thing that would bring me back weekly is not a feature. It is this: **when I paste a link
from here into my team's channel, it has to say what the thing is.** Right now every one of them
unfurls as "Resource — Learn Claude" with a URL ending `?id=r-8e6cc78d3c`, and the site's own
Copy link button is what hands me that. I am a PM. Ninety per cent of the value I get from a
directory is the moment I forward three of its links with a sentence of my own on top. This site
does the hard part — the judgement, the `Skip if:`, the honest badge — and then loses all of it at
the exact moment I try to pass it on. Fix the share and I am a repeat visitor and a distributor.
Leave it, and I am a person who read a good page once.

And while you are there: stop telling me you have not covered my role.
