# Attack 2: a product manager
Written as a product manager, 2026-09-05. Live site only:
`https://mojtaba-alehosseini.github.io/learn-claude/`. Everything below was reproduced live;
where I cite counts, I confirmed them against the shipped `data/items.js` / `data/picks.js`
the site itself loads.

---

## 1. First 60 seconds

Big serif headline: **"I'm a [role] and I've [level]."** Under it, "Find what's worth your
time." and **"635 resources, checked by hand."**

Two clicks and I am at results. That is genuinely fast — no signup, no cookie wall, no
newsletter modal. Good.

But the third thing I read on the page is the thing I stopped trusting by minute 20. **"checked
by hand"** is on the front door. The site's own methodology page says: *"Right now: 635
resources — 98 read by ai, 501 skimmed, 36 found only. Nothing is read in full yet."*
Thirty-six of them, in the site's own words, **"Nobody has looked at the content yet."** Nobody's
hand checked those. The homepage claim and the methodology page disagree, and the homepage is
the one doing the selling.

Also: a hand-drawn illustration in the hero cycles on a timer — a coffee mug, then a book, then
a node graph, then an easel. It re-renders mid-interaction. My first click on "a product
manager" was swallowed and I had to click twice. I could not reproduce the swallow reliably
enough to file it, but the cycling is real and it is decoration on a page whose entire job is
two questions.

## 2. The front door, all four levels

I answered "a product manager" (count drops to "170 resources match so far"), then each level in
turn.

| Level | Results | Picks heading | Verdict |

| never used Claude | **13** | "Start with these three" | Decent. Getting started video, DataCamp course, a Tom's Guide first-impressions piece. But "everything else" hands a total beginner *"Everyone should be using Claude Code more"* (Lenny's Newsletter, **subscription**, Published 14 Oct 2025) whose own Skip if says *"it dates from October 2025 so it predates Cowork."* Why is that in front of someone who has never opened Claude. |

The pattern: the site is built for beginners and thins out to nothing exactly where an
experienced buyer arrives. 13 -> 43 -> 56 -> 58 results, but the *quality* of curation goes 3

Front-door nit: the count "170 resources match so far" updates silently. There is **no

`document.querySelectorAll('[aria-live],[role=status]').length` -> `0`). Browse.html *does* have
one on its count. The two pages disagree about whether a changing number should be announced.

## 3. What the catalogue gives me

The card format is the best thing here. Every card carries **"For:"** and **"Skip if:"**, and
the Skip if lines are frequently better than the summaries. Real examples I read:

- *"Skip if: Your product is not instrumented — the guide says outright to stop reading and go
  set up analytics first."*
- *"Skip if: You have not yet used the free repo — start there. The sales page also opens with
  'Claude Code with Opus-4.6 is basically AGI', which is exactly the hype this space is full of,
  and the time-saving claims are unmeasured."*
- *"Skip if: It costs money and it is a study, not a method - findings about where Claude broke,
  not steps to copy."*

That is real editorial judgment. It is the only reason I stayed on the site for an hour.

**Then I looked at what "a product manager" + "built things with it" actually contains.** 58
resources:

- **53 of 58 come from one source: Anthropic Academy.** 50 of those are 15-minute "docs" pages.
- **55 of 58 are tier "Skimmed". 2 are "Found only". Exactly 1 is "Read by AI."**
- **57 of 58 have no publish date.**
- **12 of 58 have a "Who it's for" line naming a different job.** Verbatim from the cards:
  *"Sales reps prepping for a call..."* (Account research), *"Account managers who want a weekly
  health check..."* (Account tracking), *"Engineering teams tired of re-answering the same
  onboarding..."* (Build an 'Ask the Company' agent), *"In-house counsel or legal ops triaging a
  high volume of standard NDAs"* (NDA triage at scale), *"Litigation or research attorneys..."*
  (Legal research), *"Recruiters or hiring managers assembling an offer package..."* (Offer
  process), *"Buy-side equity research analysts..."* (Update your financial model after
  earnings).

So the role filter is not a role filter. It is a tag someone attached, and the card underneath
openly says it belongs to somebody else. The site's own copy — *"A link with no judgment is just
a list"* — is exactly what this cell is: a list with judgment printed next to it that contradicts
the sorting.

**Sort is broken in practice.** "Best checked first" resolves to tier -> checked-date -> title
A-Z. Because 55 of my 58 share one tier and 276 items across the catalogue share one checked date
(2026-08-29), the tiebreak collapses to the alphabet. My "Everything else for you (56)" opened,
in order: *Account research, Account tracking, Audit a folder of visual assets against your
guidelines, Build a battle card library, Build a campaign brief, Build a daily briefing across
your tools, Build customer personas, Build the competitive comparison doc, Call prep.* A-B-C.
That is not a ranking, it is a phone book with a ranking's label on it.

"Newest first" is worse: 489 of 635 items have no publish date and are given the sort key
`"0000"`. For my cell (57 of 58 undated), "Newest first" changes nothing meaningful.

**And there is no way to filter by how well something was checked.** The four-tier ladder is the
site's headline differentiator. "More filters" gives topic, format, cost and "Official Anthropic
only" — no tier. I cannot say "show me only the ones a human or an AI actually read." That is
the one filter I wanted and the one filter that does not exist.

## 4. Paths

**Yes, there is a route for me: "Product work without waiting for engineering."**
`paths.html?id=pm-without-engineering`. 5 steps, "about 2 hours", free.

I read every step reason. They are the best writing on the site and every one of them justifies
its *position*, not just its existence:

1. *"Start with the work only you do. Synthesising research is where Claude pays for itself
   fastest and it needs no setup at all."*
2. *"Second, because one good synthesis is a party trick and a repeatable one is a habit.
   Projects is where a product area stops being forty loose chats you cannot find again."*
3. *"Third: hand over the parts that repeat. This comes after Projects because delegation
   without somewhere to put the output just moves the mess."*
4. *"Fourth, and deliberately not first. Safety guidance is abstract until you know what you
   would actually delegate — which step 3 just taught you."*
5. *"This step needs a terminal, and if that is a no then stop here — the four above stand on
   their own."*

Plus the intro: *"Steps 1 to 4 need nothing but a browser — stop after step 4 and you can
already synthesise research, keep it somewhere, delegate the repeatable parts and know what is
safe to paste in."*

That is a real curriculum. Somebody thought about ordering.

**Two problems.**

First: **there are 7 paths, and every one is aimed at "never used" or "used it a little."** Not
one path exists for "used it a lot" or "built things with it" — for any of the ten roles. The PM
path's internal level is `basic`. But the paths page never prints the level. It says only *"For
a product manager."* So I — a PM who has built things — click the PM path, get a beginner path,
and nothing on the page warns me. Three role/level cells have no picks block at all either
(`student|builder`, `teacher|builder`, `writer-marketer|builder`) — those readers get silence
with no explanation.

Second: the time labels are inconsistent. The writer path reads *"4 steps · about 81 minutes ·
free"* — "about" in front of an exact odd number. Mine reads "about 2 hours" for an internal
total of 93 minutes. For a site whose stated rule is that numbers are generated, not typed,
"about 81 minutes" reads like a template that nobody looked at.

## 5. The card and the resource page

I opened three. **On the good ones, yes** — I would click through. The resource page is well
built: **What it teaches** (bulleted), **Who it's for**, **Skip it if**, **Before this**
(prerequisites — genuinely useful; the Every guide lists *"Product must be instrumented with
analytics"*), **How we checked this one** with the tier explained in plain words, and a big
"Open on Every" button.

Then I hit `resource.html?id=r-cd019b4ce7` — **"Building a Claude Project for Teaching &
Learning (recording)"**, Northeastern University. One page, three claims that cannot all be
true:

- Badge and method block: **"Found only. We found it and sorted it. Nobody has looked at the
  content yet."**
- Summary: *"A recorded university session on designing a Claude Project that acts as a course
  assistant - what to load into project knowledge, how to write the project instructions, and
  how students then interact with it. The page bundles the recording with a downloadable handout
  and a faculty FAQ..."* — plus three "What it teaches" bullets.
- Skip it if: *"the recording is labelled 2025, so the Projects screens and features shown are
  behind what you will see today."*
- Date line: **"No publish date given."**

Nobody looked at it — but we know its contents in detail, we know it ships with a handout and a
faculty FAQ, and we read the year off it. And having read the year off it, the card still says
no publish date is given. Pick one.

Worse: this item is flagged `status: "outdated"` in the site's own data. The rendering code only
handles `status === "dead"`. **The site knows three resources are outdated and shows the reader
nothing.** The other two are *"Building effective agents"* (Anthropic) and *"Craft a Professional
AI Developer Setup (Cursor & Claude Code)."*

Smaller: **"Back to browse" is a bare `href="browse.html"` with no query string.** I came from
`browse.html?role=pm&level=builder`. The site's own back affordance throws my filters away and
dumps me in all 635. The browser back button works; the button the site built for me is worse
than the one it did not.

## 6. The picks block — two, and the wrong two

`browse.html?role=pm&level=builder` -> **58 resources** -> **"Start with these two — picked by AI
· 31 Aug 2026."**

**Does two read as honesty or as a broken feature?** As broken. Here is why.

The site has 37 pick cells. 35 hold three. Two hold two: `pm|builder` and
`non-technical|builder`. Both were picked on **31 Aug 2026**. Every other cell was picked on 4 or
5 Sep. The only two short cells are the two oldest. That is not restraint, that is an unfinished
pass with a heading that adapts to hide it.

Honesty would be a sentence. There is not one. No "we only found two worth naming from 58." No
"the third candidate did not clear the bar." The heading silently swaps the numeral and moves on.
And the machinery goes further: if a cell ever drops to one pick, the block **deletes itself
entirely** with no message at all. A feature that vanishes rather than explains is not being
honest with me.

**Now the two picks themselves.**

**Pick 1 — "Agent-native Product Management (Every's guide)"** (Every, article, 1 hour, free,
"Read by AI"). Reason: *"The only candidate about changing how the job works rather than doing
one task faster - and it refuses readers honestly, telling anyone without instrumentation to stop
reading and go fix analytics first."* This is a good pick and a good reason. It adds something
the title cannot: it tells me the guide will reject me if my product is not instrumented. That
is judgment.

**Pick 2 — "Build an 'Ask the Company' agent"** (Anthropic Academy, docs, 15 min, free,
"Skimmed"). Its own "Who it's for", printed directly under the reason: **"Engineering teams tired
of re-answering the same onboarding and ownership questions."** Its summary on the resource page:
*"An internal ask-anything agent **for engineers**..."*

One of exactly two things this site tells a product manager to start with is a page it admits it
only skimmed, that says on its face it is for engineers. The pick reason claims *"Fifty-three
single-workflow pages in this pool, and this is the one that builds something the whole team uses
daily."* If 53 of 58 are single-workflow Academy pages and only one of them is worth naming, the
honest headline is not "Start with these two" — it is "we do not have good material for you yet."

Also on the same screen: the reason says **"Fifty-three"** and the heading right below says
**"Everything else for you (56)."** Two numbers about the same pool, on the same screen, that do
not agree.

**Do the reasons add something the title did not?** Mixed, and it splits cleanly by level. At
`basic` and `confident` they are excellent — *"quality drops past ten interviews and it says
so"*, *"a non-developer's real terminal setup demonstrated rather than described"*, *"the largest
capability jump per minute in the pool."* At `builder`, pick 2's reason is doing PR for something
the card underneath contradicts. Reasons range 134-325 characters and read as written, not
templated. Real judgment — applied unevenly.

**Every single pick across the whole site — all 109 of them — is tier "Skimmed" (81) or "Read by
AI" (28).** Not one recommendation on this site comes from something a person read in full.
"Start with these three" means "start with three things we mostly skimmed."

## 7. Dates and tier badges

**Dates: 489 of 635 (77%) say "No publish date given."** Of those, 445 give me nothing else at
all — no updated date, no signal, nothing. The other 44 print the odd pairing: **"No publish date
given"** and, on the next line, **"Updated 1 Jul 2026."**

I understand the intent — do not invent a date you do not have. As a reader it still reads as a
shrug. And for my cell it is total: **57 of 58 PM/builder resources have no publish date.** I
cannot tell maintained from abandoned for 98% of what the site offers me.

**The worse behaviour: when a real date exists and is old, the site deletes it.** 17 cards render
*"Published over a year ago — may not match Claude today"* **instead of** the date. That string is
doing duty for:

- *"Guidance on AI detection, and why we're disabling Turnitin's"* — actually **16 Aug 2023**.
  Three years old.
- *"AI prompt engineering: A deep dive"* — 5 Sep 2024.
- *"Building effective agents"* — 19 Dec 2024 (and separately flagged `outdated` in the data,
  which the card never says).
- *"Mastering Claude Code in 30 minutes"* — 22 May 2025.
- *"Lesson 1: Introduction to AI Fluency"* and *"Lesson 2B: The 4D Framework"* — both 12 Jun 2025.

A 2023 document and a 15-month-old document get the identical string. The homepage promises **"We
show the date."** It hides the date at precisely the moment the date decides whether I open the
tab. Thirteen months versus three years is the difference between "worth a look" and "delete it."

Three items also carry month-only dates ("Updated May 2026", "Updated Jun 2026", "Updated Feb
2026"). Fine — I would rather have that than an invented day.

**Tier badges: "Skimmed" 501, "Read by AI" 98, "Found only" 36, "Read in full" 0.**

What they say about the work behind this: it is a harvest, not a review. **537 of 635 (85%) are
Skimmed or Found only.** The methodology page's own opening line is **"We would rather have 70
resources we can vouch for than 700 we cannot."** They shipped 635 they cannot vouch for. The
principle and the inventory point in opposite directions on the same page.

I will say this for the badges: they are honest, they are visible on every card and every
resource page, and the tooltips do not hedge — *"Nobody has looked at the content yet"* is a hard
thing to print about your own product. That honesty is real. It just does not survive contact
with "635 resources, checked by hand" on the front page.

document. The file's own header claims *"keyword matching alone puts the right resource first in
seven of them"* — out of eight benchmark sentences the author wrote. Eight self-authored sentences
is not a search evaluation. Five real PM queries gave me one clean win, two partials, and two
failures.

**Gemini** wrote for every resource."* Nothing on any reader-facing page says which AI wrote what.
"picked by AI", "Read by AI" — never which one, never that a different vendor's model authored
the fields that decide what I find.

Cosmetic but telling: the browser tab reads **"Browse 1 Claude resources — Learn Claude"** on a
one-result page. The visible count line correctly says "1 resource for...". The title never
singularises.

## 10. Keyboard walk

I walked `browse.html?role=pm&level=builder` and `resource.html?id=r-ef99edbbfd` with Tab only.

**What works.** Focus rings are visible and deliberate — a clean outline on the "Browse" nav link
and on the filter options. The CSS goes further than most: the card outline is drawn on the whole
card when the title link is focused, and there is a scoped fix so focusing a card's CTA does not
draw two rings at once. There is a real "Skip to content" link. High-contrast mode is handled.
Filter buttons carry `role="checkbox"` with correct `aria-checked`, and the check glyph is
`aria-hidden` so it does not pollute the name. The resource page is a tidy **13 stops** end to
end. Someone did an accessibility pass and it shows.

**What does not.**

**Browse: 29 Tab stops before the first result.** 90 focusable elements on the page. The order is:
Skip to content -> wordmark -> 3 nav -> Clear all -> 10 role checkboxes -> 4 level -> 4 time ->
More filters -> Search -> Sort -> 2 applied chips -> *then* the first resource link. The skip link
targets `#main`, which sits **before** the filter rail, so it saves four stops and leaves all 24
filter controls in my way. There is no second skip to results. Keyboard users pay a 24-key toll on
every visit.

**Home page: focus is stranded on an invisible control.** Measured directly. Focus "a product
manager", activate it. Afterwards:

```
activeText: "a product manager"
rect: "0x0"          offsetParentNull: true
liveRegions: 0       countText: "170 resources match so far"
```

Keyboard focus stays on a button with a zero-by-zero bounding box that is no longer rendered.
Meanwhile the question above it silently changed from "Who are you?" to "How much Claude do you
know?", the count changed to 170, and **there is no live region on the page to announce either.** A
sighted mouse user sees the page transform. A keyboard or screen-reader user gets: nothing moved,
nothing was said, and my focus ring is now nowhere. That is the *front door* — the one interaction
the entire site funnels through.

## 11. How we check

**Less.** And that surprised me, because the page is well written.

What it earns:
- *"Four levels, and we do not round up."*
- *"390 of those 635 (61%) are Anthropic's own — official just means who published it, not how
  good it is."*
- *"We do not take money to rank a resource. We are not affiliated with Anthropic."*
- The certification section: *"Anthropic runs a real certification, but it is open to members of
  the Claude Partner Network, not to the public. A lot of sites sell preparation for an exam most
  readers cannot sit."* That is a real service to a reader.
- *"It needs a GitHub account, which is a real barrier and we know it."* Naming your own barrier
  is rare.
- The numbers on the page match the shipped data exactly. They are generated, not typed.

What destroys it:

1. **The page opens with "We would rather have 70 resources we can vouch for than 700 we cannot"
   and closes with a link to "Browse the 635 resources."** It states a principle and then reports
   having done the opposite, in the same column of text, without noticing.
2. **"What we do: We find material, read it, and write two things."** They did not read it. 501
   skimmed, 36 not opened. That sentence is false for 85% of the catalogue, and the paragraph two
   screens down proves it.
3. **The word "pick" does not appear on this page.** The single most editorially loaded feature on
   the site — "Start with these three", the block that decides what 635 resources collapse into —
   has no method statement anywhere. Who picked, from what pool, against what bar, why some cells
   get two and three get none. The page whose entire job is explaining the method skips the one
   part where judgment is actually being exercised.
4. **It never names the AI.** "Read by AI", "picked by AI" — which model, which prompt, checked by
   whom. And the shipped search code says the fields that drive every query were written by
   Gemini. That is not on this page or any other.

I trust the tier badges more after reading it. I trust the front page less, and I trust the
recommendations less, because I now know none of them came from anything a person read.

## 12. Everything broken, ranked

**S1 — The role filter contradicts the cards inside it.** `browse.html?role=pm&level=builder`. I
filtered to "a product manager". 12 of the 58 results have a "Who it's for" naming another job —
*"Sales reps prepping for a call"*, *"Account managers who want a weekly health check"*,
*"In-house counsel or legal ops triaging a high volume of standard NDAs"*, *"Litigation or
research attorneys"*, *"Recruiters or hiring managers assembling an offer package"*, *"Buy-side
equity research analysts"*. A reasonable person expects a role filter to return that role's
material. **Harms:** every experienced user of the busiest filter combination; they conclude the
tagging is noise and stop trusting the other filters too.

**S1 — One of only two PM "start here" picks is for engineers, and was only skimmed.** Same URL.
**"Start with these two"** -> pick 2, *"Build an 'Ask the Company' agent"*, badge **Skimmed**,
"Who it's for": **"Engineering teams tired of re-answering the same onboarding and ownership
questions."** Resource page (`resource.html?id=r-819e268d62`) summary: *"An internal ask-anything
agent for engineers."* **Harms:** the highest-intent visitor the site gets — someone who answered
both questions honestly and is ready to act.

**S1 — The homepage claim is contradicted by the methodology page.** Home: **"635 resources,
checked by hand."** How we check: **"Nothing is read in full yet"**, 36 items *"Nobody has looked
at the content yet."* **Harms:** everyone; it is the site's core trust claim, and the refutation
is two clicks away in the site's own voice.

**S2 — Real publication dates are deleted when they matter most.** 17 cards print *"Published over
a year ago — may not match Claude today"* in place of the date. Behind that string: 16 Aug 2023, 5
Sep 2024, 19 Dec 2024, 22 May 2025, 12 Jun 2025. Against a homepage promise that reads **"We show
the date."** **Harms:** anyone triaging by age — a three-year-old doc and a fifteen-month-old doc
look identical.

**S2 — Search fails core PM vocabulary and splits on spelling.** `?q=prioritisation` -> 1 result (a
Claude Code skills repo). `?q=prioritization` -> 1 result (*"Work through grant options in chat"*,
for grant administrators). `?q=stakeholder+update` -> top three include **"Proposed Revisions to
SPJ's Code of Ethics (2026)"** and **"Update your financial model after earnings"**. **Harms:**
every non-US reader, and every reader who types a job word instead of a product word — which is
exactly the behaviour the search box invites: *"Title, topic, or what you want to do."*

**S2 — Keyboard focus is stranded on a 0x0 element at the front door, with no announcement.**
`index.html`. Choose a role: `activeElement` remains the now-unrendered chip (`rect: 0x0`,
`offsetParent: null`), the question is replaced, the count changes to 170, and
`[aria-live],[role=status]` count on the page is **0**. Browse.html has a live region for the same
job. **Harms:** keyboard and screen-reader users, at the single mandatory entry point.

**S2 — "Best checked first" degrades to alphabetical.** `browse.html?role=pm&level=builder`. After
the picks: *Account research, Account tracking, Audit a folder..., Build a battle card library,
Build a campaign brief, Build a daily briefing..., Build customer personas, Build the competitive
comparison doc, Call prep.* 55 of 58 share one tier, 276 catalogue items share one checked date, so
the label describes a sort that is not happening. **Harms:** anyone who reads past the picks and
assumes position means quality.

**S3 — "Start with these two" with no explanation, and vanishing blocks.** 35 of 37 cells hold
three picks. The two that hold two were both picked 31 Aug 2026, the oldest date in the file; every
other cell is 4-5 Sep. Nothing on screen explains the shortfall. At one pick the block deletes
itself silently. Three cells have no picks block at all. **Harms:** readers in the four thin cells,
who cannot tell "we found nothing" from "this is broken."

**S3 — Three resources are flagged `outdated` in the data and the cards never say so.**
`resource.html?id=r-cd019b4ce7` renders "No publish date given" and no warning; also *"Building
effective agents"* and *"Craft a Professional AI Developer Setup."* The rendering only handles

has looked at the content yet."* Summary: bundles *"a downloadable handout and a faculty FAQ."*

## 13. The one thing that would make me leave

**The role filter lying, on the exact combination I selected.**

I answered the two questions honestly — product manager, built things with it — which is the most
engaged thing a stranger can do on this site. What came back was two picks, one of which says under
its own headline **"Engineering teams tired of re-answering the same onboarding and ownership
questions"**, followed by 56 more, sorted alphabetically, 53 of them from one publisher, 55 of them
skimmed, 57 of them undated, and a dozen of them openly labelled for sales reps, in-house counsel
and recruiters.

The site's whole promise is *"A link with no judgment is just a list, and lists are what made this
hard in the first place."* On my cell, it is a list. Worse than a list — it is a list that printed
judgment next to each item and the judgment says *this one is not for you*, and the site put it
there anyway.

The homepage says "checked by hand." I now know the highest-confidence recommendation on my page
was skimmed and mis-targeted. Everything else I read becomes something I have to re-check myself,
which is the exact work the site exists to save me. I close the tab.

## 14. What is genuinely good

- **"Skip if:" is the best idea on the site**, and the lines earn it: *"Skip it if you will not
  subscribe to Every - the free portion stops at the roadmap section, and the three most specific
  claims sit behind the paywall."* Almost nobody publishes that about a resource they are
  recommending.
- **The paths.** *"Fourth, and deliberately not first. Safety guidance is abstract until you know
  what you would actually delegate."* Every step reason explains its **position**. This is real
  instructional design and it is rare.
- **The tier ladder itself** — with tooltips that do not flatter: *"Nobody has looked at the
  content yet."* Publishing "0 read in full" instead of quietly inflating is a hard call made
  correctly.
- **PM + "used it a little"** is a genuinely excellent cell. Three picks, all right, all justified.
- **Speed.** Client-side, instant, no spinner, no account, no tracking, and a footer that says so.
- **Resource pages are well structured** — "Before this" prerequisites are a detail most
  directories skip.
- **Real accessibility work exists**: skip link, visible focus rings, `aria-checked` on the filter
  checkboxes, an `aria-live` count on Browse, high-contrast handling, and a card-level focus outline
  that is scoped so it does not double up.
- **"61% are Anthropic's own"** stated plainly on the methodology page, unprompted.

The judgment is real. The plumbing under it is not finished, and the front page is writing cheques
the methodology page bounces.
