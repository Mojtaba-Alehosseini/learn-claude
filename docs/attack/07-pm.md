# Attack: a product manager
Written as someone who is a product manager, at 2026-08-27. Site version: 22e4532.

I have one hour between a roadmap review and a 1:1. I want two things: something I can
use myself this week, and something I can send my engineers without looking naive.

---

## 1. The first 60 seconds

The front door is one sentence with two blanks: *"I'm [a role] and I've [level]."* I
understood it instantly. That is the best thing on the site and I want to say so before
I start taking it apart.

I picked **a product manager**. I picked **never used Claude** — because that is true,
I have watched demos, I have not opened it. The line under the button said **"3
resources match so far."**

Three. Out of 353.

I did not believe it, so I clicked **Show me**. It was true. Three cards.

Then I did what I actually do when a directory disappoints me: I typed my real question
into the box on the home page — *"how do I write a PRD with this"* — and pressed Show
me. The page came back:

> **No match for "how do I write a PRD with this".**
> Try fewer words, or browse by role instead.

and above it, in bold:

> **0 resources for a product manager**

That sentence is wrong. There are 56. I will come back to why in 9.1 and 9.2, because
it is not a content problem, it is a bug, and it is the reason I nearly closed the tab
inside two minutes.

`resource.html?id=r-66cc1560b7`. The page gives me three chips: **course · several days

"Pay once." That is what the site tells me a $3,000 course costs. The same three words

## 6. Search, in my words

Reproduced with `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 python scripts/test-search.py "<sentence>"`,
which the script's own docstring says "mirrors assets/js/search.js exactly".

| my sentence | ranked index says | what the site actually gave me |
|---|---|---|
| how do I write a PRD with this | 9 results, top: **Write a PRD with Claude Code (Module 2.1)** | **0** |
| what do I tell my engineers | 4 results, top: *Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing* | **0** |
| is this worth the money for a team of 12 | 23 results, top: *Claude for Small Business setup* | **0** |

The middle row is its own small insult: I asked what to tell my engineers and the best
answer in the index is a Wikipedia page about spotting AI writing, filed under
writer-marketer. Fine. At least it is an answer.

But the "0" column is the real story, and it is a bug, not a ranking problem.

`assets/js/browse.js:265` is the **only** place in the codebase that calls
`window.LCSearch.load()`, and it sits inside the `input` event handler on Browse's own
search box. If you type your sentence on the **home page** and press "Show me", you
arrive at `browse.html?q=…` and the index is never requested. `rank()` returns `null`
(`search.js`: *"or null when the index is not ready"*), and `matches()` falls back to
the comment on `browse.js:86` — *"fall back to plain substring so typing is never
dead"* — which requires **every** word of my sentence, including "how", "do", "I",
"with", "this", to appear literally in title + summary + who_for + source.

No English sentence survives that. Verified against the live site, in a tab of my own:

```
https://mojtaba-alehosseini.github.io/learn-claude/browse.html?role=pm&q=how%20do%20I%20write%20a%20PRD%20with%20this
→ { "title": "Browse 0 Claude resources — Learn Claude",
    "count": "0 resources for a product manager",
    "empty": "No match for “how do I write a PRD with this”. Try fewer words, or browse by role instead.",
    "cards": 0,
    "searchIndexReady": false,
    "indexScriptRequested": false }
```

`indexScriptRequested: false`. The 255 KB index the whole search design is built on was
never even asked for. The home page has a search box whose only function is to produce
a wrong answer.

Deployed JS confirmed byte-identical to local:

```
$ curl -s .../assets/js/browse.js | diff - assets/js/browse.js  → IDENTICAL
   (same for search.js, home.js, paths.js)
```

## 9. Everything that is broken, ranked

**9.1 — The home page's search box always returns zero, and the search index is never
loaded on that path.** `assets/js/browse.js:265` is the only `LCSearch.load()` call in
the codebase and it lives inside the `input` handler. Arriving at `browse.html?q=…`
from the home form (`home.js:298-305` sets `p.set("q", q)` and navigates) leaves
`index === null`, so `rank()` returns null and `matches()` falls back to whole-word
substring AND (`browse.js:86-88`). Live check on my own tab returned
`searchIndexReady: false`, `indexScriptRequested: false`, `cards: 0` for all three of
my sentences. The ranked index would have returned 9, 4 and 23. Worst finding on the
site, because the home page invites the query and then destroys it.

**9.2 — With a query and a role, the count line states a falsehood: "0 resources for a
product manager".** `browse.js:172-176`: `onlyRole` is computed from the six filter
axes and **never looks at `q`**, so a search that matched nothing still takes the
role-summary branch. The site has 56 for me. It told me nought. Verified live.

**9.3 — The never-used shelf is 3 cards, none of them about product management, and one
of the three is paywalled.** `role-view.py pm never-used`. The three `who_for` lines
address "Someone opening Claude for the first time", "Anyone who wants shared words",
and "Non-technical professionals". Zero are `only pm`
(`python -c "…roles==['pm']…"` → all three shared). Cost of card 3: `subscription`.

**9.4 — Eleven of thirteen paid resources show no price anywhere, and the site is
holding the prices in a field it never renders.**
`Counter(cost) → {free: 42, subscription: 7, paid-once: 6, free-account: 1}`. Three pm
records carry `"Price on 2026-08-21: $…"` in `notes`; `grep -n "notes" assets/js/*.js`
returns only two unrelated tooltip strings, so `notes` is dead data. The chip vocabulary
is `LC.COST` in `ui.js:32-35`: "pay once" covers $49 and $3,000 identically. There is a
Cost filter axis (`browse.js:22`) and it can only ask "free or not", which is the
question I care least about.

**9.5 — "What it teaches" is un-capitalised machine output on every one of the 353
resource pages.** Counted: 353 of 353 have `teaches[]` bullets starting lower case; 46
of 46 pm items with prerequisites have the same in "Before this". Live from
`resource.html?id=r-66cc1560b7`: *"build and ship ai products using claude code"*.
From the Claude Code docs page: *"write an effective compact claude md file"* —
`CLAUDE.md` mangled. It is the first section a visitor reads after the summary, and it
makes a hand-curated site look auto-generated. **I have not seen this raised anywhere.**

**9.6 — My shelf is a Claude Code shelf wearing a PM label.** 36 of 56 tagged
`claude-code`; **24 of the 28** cards at "used it a lot"; 23 of 56 titles contain the
literal string "Claude Code". A directory that answers "how do I learn Claude as a PM"
with "install a terminal tool" has picked one answer and hidden the question.

**9.7 — Two of my seven repo cards are tier "Found only" — nobody opened them — and one
of those two tells me not to use it.** `role-view.py pm builder`. Quoted in §3. A PM
does not audit a GitHub repo before running it; that is the whole reason a curated
directory has value. Shipping an unread autonomous-agent repo to the least
code-literate technical role on the site is the one entry here that could actually cost
someone their job.

**9.8 — I am given exactly 2 `docs` cards and one of them is addressed to developers.**
00-facts: pm docs = 2. **Best practices for Claude Code**, `who_for`: *"Developers who
installed Claude Code, use it daily, and still get mediocre results."* So my real docs
count for my role is one: the official Product Management plugin page.

**9.9 — `paths.html?role=pm` silently drops the role and shows three routes for other
people.** `grep "role" assets/js/paths.js` → nothing; the file only reads `?id`. Live
check: `mentionsPM: false`, lede *"A short list, in order. Start at the top."* The
missing path is known. The missing *acknowledgement* is not: there is no empty state,
no "closest thing", no link back to Browse for my role.

**9.10 — 20 of 56 `who_for` lines never name a product manager, and 3 name a different
job.** Regex over `data/items.json` (shown in §3). "a product manager" is a real
audience for 36 cards and a dumping ground for the other 20.

**9.11 — On a 390px phone, 64% of the first screen is chrome before a single card.**
Measured in a real 390×844 frame: `firstCardTopPx: 538`, `viewportH: 841`, plus a 69px
fixed bar; 234px of a 467px card is visible. The Search field and Sort dropdown sit
above the results re-asking a question the home page already asked me.

**9.12 — 22 of my 56 have no publish date** (`role-view.py pm --counts` → `no date: 22`),
in a role where nine of the top titles are about a product that shipped Cowork this
year. Site-wide version of this is already known; the sharpening is that it is 39% of
*my* shelf, concentrated in exactly the courses and repos I would have to pay for.

## 11. What is genuinely good (be honest, but brief)

- **The `Skip if:` line is the best idea in this category.** Nobody else does it. The
  Product School one — *"You are paying yourself. $2,999 buys the same ground as Carl
  Vellotti's free course plus the Anthropic plugin"* — is worth more to me than the
  other 55 cards put together.
- **The tier badges are honest.** "Skimmed", "Found only", "Read by AI". A directory
  that admits it only skimmed something is trustworthy in a way that a five-star rating
  never is.
- **The two-blank sentence on the front page.** I understood it without reading a word
  of instruction, and the count updates before I commit. That is good product work.
- **The count is exact and never rounded**, and when it drops it offers a way out. The
  intent in `renderCount` is right — it is only the query case that leaks (9.2).
- **Nothing is tracked and nothing needs an account.** *"We do not track your progress.
  Nothing here needs an account."* I would send that line to my privacy reviewer with no
  edits.
- **The phone layout does not break.** No overflow, thumb-reachable filters, 44px tap
  targets in the footer. It is too tall (9.11), but it is not broken, and that is rarer
  than it should be.
